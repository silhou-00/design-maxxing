# Capture — UX Planet: 50 Design Styles Every Designer Should Know for Better Prompting

**Source:** https://uxplanet.org/50-design-styles-every-designer-should-know-for-better-prompting-56c09d55db62
**Author:** Himanshu Bhardwaj · **Published:** 1 Aug 2025 · 17 min read
**Captured:** 2026-09-03, Chrome via `get_page_text`
**Structure:** each entry = short description (origin + intent) + Core Elements + Mood & Occasion.
**Images:** 1 plate per style, `miro.medium.com`. Those relevant to the 12 focus styles are

> Author's framing: "Design styles are more than just aesthetic categories. They are complete
> ecosystems, shaped by culture, history, materials, mood, and form." The list is built for AI
> prompting — "AI doesn't understand ambiguity. What it needs is clarity. What it understands is
> style names."

Focus styles from this source are marked **◆**.

---

**1. Neoclassical** — Rooted in the grandeur of classical antiquity. Revives Greco-Roman ideals like order, symmetry, and refined elegance.
· *Core:* Serif fonts, columns, laurel wreaths, marble textures, gold accents, symmetry, muted palettes.
· *Mood:* Formal, timeless, noble. Luxury brands, museums, heritage products, academic publications.

**2. Baroque** — Opulent, emotional, dramatic. 17th-century Europe; thrives on excess.
· *Core:* Flourishes, deep shadows, gold leaf, religious or mythological motifs, rich textiles, high contrast.
· *Mood:* Regal, ornate, celebratory. Event invitations, premium packaging, lavish editorial.

**3. Aurora** — Inspired by the northern lights. Flowing, iridescent gradients and dreamy abstractions.
· *Core:* Iridescent gradients, blur effects, soft glows, translucent overlays, organic wave forms.
· *Mood:* Dreamy, futuristic, meditative. Wellness apps, tech product launches, spiritual themes.

**4. Ethereal** — Delicacy and transcendence; weightless, otherworldly.
· *Core:* Light pastels, gauzy overlays, minimal serif fonts, low contrast, feathered shadows.
· *Mood:* Calm, mystical, romantic. Spiritual brands, high fashion, mindfulness interfaces.

**5. Filigree** — Intricate, delicate ornamentation, usually gold or silver.
· *Core:* Delicate line patterns, metallic inks, curves, lace textures, monogram fonts.
· *Mood:* Luxurious, vintage, ceremonial. Heritage branding, editorial layouts, luxury packaging.

**6. Acanthus** — Classical architectural ornamentation from the acanthus plant; Corinthian columns.
· *Core:* Leaf motifs, flowing curves, symmetrical vines, stone textures, natural greens and golds.
· *Mood:* Regal, organic, classical. Wedding invites, art museum branding, premium product design.

**7. Anthropomorphic** — Human traits given to non-human forms.
· *Core:* Expressive eyes, limbs on objects, character-based icons, rounded friendly shapes.
· *Mood:* Fun, quirky, friendly. Children's products, gamified UX, approachable branding.

**8. Pixel Art** — Nostalgic; early computer and video game graphics, visible pixels.
· *Core:* Grid-based visuals, low-res icons, limited color palettes, 8-bit/16-bit look.
· *Mood:* Nostalgic, geeky, fun. Indie games, retro-themed events, creative portfolios.

**9. Conceptual Sketch** ◆ *(→ hand-drawn)* — Mimics rough hand-drawn sketches; spontaneity over polish. "Behind-the-scenes, raw creative feel."
· *Core:* Pencil/ink lines, crosshatching, greyscale tones, annotations, sketch paper texture.
· *Mood:* Experimental, informal, idea-driven. Portfolios, design journals, brainstorming tools.

**10. Luxury Typography** ◆ *(→ editorial)* — Refined letterforms carry the identity alone; minimalist layout + expressive serifs.
· *Core:* Serif fonts, high letter spacing, gold foil, monochrome palettes, bespoke ligatures.
· *Mood:* Elegant, elite, timeless. Luxury fashion, beauty brands, high-end editorial.

**11. Japandi** ◆ *(→ minimalism)* — Japanese minimalism fused with Scandinavian functionality. Simplicity, craftsmanship, nature-inspired serenity. "In UI, calm, decluttered interfaces with neutral tones and soft curves."
· *Core:* Light woods, beige/gray palettes, clean sans-serif fonts, minimal icons, natural materials.
· *Mood:* Calm, peaceful, intentional. Lifestyle brands, home decor, meditation/wellness apps.

**12. Memphis** — Bold, loud, unapologetically fun. 1980s; throws rules out the window.
· *Core:* Squiggles, bright primaries, grids, clashing colors, block shapes, cartoonish motifs.
· *Mood:* Youthful, quirky, anti-establishment. Creative brands, event posters, retro fashion.

**13. Bohemian** — Artistic freedom and earthy charm; eclectic, globally inspired, layered.
· *Core:* Rich textures, mandalas, jewel tones, ethnic prints, script fonts, organic materials.
· *Mood:* Free, soulful, eclectic. Handmade products, travel brands, artisanal markets.

**14. Shabby Chic** — Vintage charm with feminine elegance; lived-in yet curated.
· *Core:* Whitewashed textures, faded florals, cursive typography, lace, soft pinks and blues.
· *Mood:* Romantic, cozy, nostalgic. Weddings, vintage boutiques, home decor.

**15. Farmhouse / Cottagecore** — Romanticized rural life; simplicity, nature, slow living.
· *Core:* Florals, gingham, wood textures, antique finishes, serif or hand-drawn type.
· *Mood:* Cozy, nostalgic, wholesome. Lifestyle blogs, recipe sites, rustic home brands.

**16. Victorian** — Grand, elaborate, deeply ornate; 19th-century British monarchy.
· *Core:* Ornate serif fonts, damask patterns, deep reds and greens, gilded frames, heavy florals.
· *Mood:* Opulent, historical, romantic. Book covers, tea brands, vintage fashion, museum events.

**17. Art Deco** — Sleek, geometric, sophisticated; 1920s glamour. Streamlined modernism + luxurious materials.
· *Core:* Gold accents, sunbursts, symmetry, angular shapes, rich jewel tones, sans-serifs with flair.
· *Mood:* Glamorous, upscale, jazzy. Luxury brands, cocktail events, retro-modern interfaces.

**18. Art Nouveau** — Nature-inspired, fluid, highly decorative; organic floral and botanical forms.
· *Core:* Curving lines, floral motifs, hand-lettered type, earthy palettes, stained glass patterns.
· *Mood:* Romantic, poetic, organic. Artistic brands, natural cosmetics, artisan packaging.

**19. Mystical Western** — Cowboy westerns fused with occult mysticism; desert + celestial.
· *Core:* Tarot symbols, cacti, western serif fonts, sun/moon motifs, earthy tones, leather textures.
· *Mood:* Mystical, rugged, folklore-driven. Crystal shops, tarot apps, moody music visuals.

**20. Kitsch** — Embraces "bad taste" ironically; exaggerated, sentimental pop-culture visuals.
· *Core:* Clashing prints, outdated fonts, shiny plastic textures, retro advertising visuals, excess.
· *Mood:* Campy, fun, ironic. Parody work, Gen Z fashion, nostalgic remixes.

**21. Y2K** — Late-1990s/early-2000s tech culture; cyber-futurism + millennial pop.
· *Core:* Chrome textures, pixel fonts, metallic finishes, iridescent gradients, matrix grids.
· *Mood:* Futuristic, nostalgic, edgy. Fashion branding, tech revival themes, throwback interfaces.

**22. Bauhaus** ◆ *(→ modernism)* — Functional, geometric, minimal. German design school of the 1920s; "form follows function." "It's the birth of modernist design."
· *Core:* Grid systems, sans-serif fonts, red-blue-yellow palette, circles/triangles/squares, minimal text.
· *Mood:* Rational, structured, modern. Architecture, product design, clean brand systems.

**23. Brutalism** ◆ — Raw, bold, purposefully unrefined. Borrowed from architecture; "rejects polish for stark functionality and visual honesty. In digital, it breaks UI norms to stand out."
· *Core:* Monospaced fonts, grayscale palettes, harsh edges, solid blocks, default buttons.
· *Mood:* Bold, disruptive, honest. Artist portfolios, experimental interfaces, counterculture brands.

**24. Cybercore** — High-tech, hyper-modern; hacker aesthetics, sci-fi, underground internet.
· *Core:* Neon lights, glitch effects, code patterns, green/black themes, tech symbology.
· *Mood:* Futuristic, chaotic, dystopian. Tech events, hacking culture, gaming visuals.

**25. Synthwave** — Retro-futurist; 1980s sci-fi and synth music. "'Tron' meets 'Blade Runner' with a pink-purple glow."
· *Core:* Grid horizons, VHS textures, neon gradients, 3D chrome type, purple and cyan palettes.
· *Mood:* Retro-futuristic, vibrant, dreamy. Music visuals, arcade interfaces, nostalgia apps.

**26. Vaporwave** — Surreal, satirical; critiques consumerism while celebrating 80s/90s nostalgia.
· *Core:* Pastel pinks and purples, VHS glitch, Greek busts, MS Paint elements, Japanese text, lo-fi textures.
· *Mood:* Ironic, dreamy, nostalgic. Indie music, visual art zines, anti-establishment design.

**27. Pop Art** — Mass media, advertising, comic books. Bold, colorful, attention-grabbing; 1950s–60s.
· *Core:* Ben-Day dots, bold outlines, primary colors, comic sans or display fonts, speech bubbles.
· *Mood:* Loud, fun, energetic. Pop culture campaigns, retail packaging, fashion visuals.

**28. Bento Box** ◆ — "Bento UI is a trend in interface design that presents content in neatly organized, block-like sections — like compartments in a bento box. It makes complex layouts feel digestible and modern."
· *Core:* Rounded modular blocks, subtle shadows, icons with labels, micro-animations, neutral palettes.
· *Mood:* Organized, friendly, clean. Dashboards, portfolios, productivity tools.

**29. Graffiti** — Rebellious, raw, street culture; loud and expressive.
· *Core:* Spray paint textures, dripping letters, concrete backgrounds, neon colors, freestyle typography.
· *Mood:* Urban, defiant, expressive. Streetwear brands, music festivals, youth campaigns.

**30. Tenebrism** — Dramatic lighting, intense light/dark contrast.
· *Core:* High contrast chiaroscuro, black backgrounds, spotlighting, oil textures.
· *Mood:* Intense, emotional, moody. Gothic art, storytelling visuals, cinematic posters.

**31. Gothic** — Medieval architecture and dark romanticism.
· *Core:* Blackletter fonts, stained glass textures, stone motifs, deep purples and blacks.
· *Mood:* Dark, dramatic, historic. Fantasy book covers, music posters, alternative fashion.

**32. Pointillism** — Small dots of colour forming an image; texture and optical mixing.
· *Core:* Dot clusters, optical mixing, pastel or impressionist palettes, fade transitions.
· *Mood:* Artistic, textured, tranquil. Fine art branding, creative editorials, educational content.

**33. Mixed Media** ◆ *(→ editorial)* — Blends photography, illustration, textures into one cohesive piece; layers and depth.
· *Core:* Cutouts, overlays, analog + digital collage, hand-drawn + photo elements, paper textures.
· *Mood:* Eclectic, expressive, avant-garde. Magazines, experimental branding, art education.

**34. Steampunk** — Victorian aesthetics + industrial-age machinery + sci-fi fantasy.
· *Core:* Copper tones, cogwheels, leather, top hats, serif type, steam and smoke motifs.
· *Mood:* Adventurous, vintage-futuristic, imaginative. Games, books, cosplay visuals.

**35. Kawaii** ◆ *(→ hand-drawn)* — Japanese culture of cuteness; childlike, cheerful.
· *Core:* Pastel colors, rounded icons, baby faces, blushing cheeks, handwritten type.
· *Mood:* Sweet, innocent, joyful. Stationery, kids' products, whimsical branding.

**36. Coquette** — Ultra-feminine and flirty with vintage undertones; early-2000s romantic.
· *Core:* Baby pinks, pearls, ribbon motifs, dainty serif fonts, heart shapes.
· *Mood:* Delicate, girly, nostalgic. Beauty brands, fashion reels, romantic lifestyle blogs.

**37. Surrealism** — Breaks logic and realism; dreamlike juxtapositions.
· *Core:* Collage, disjointed objects, melting forms, metaphors, unexpected scale.
· *Mood:* Dreamy, unsettling, thought-provoking. Concept art, experimental visuals, literary design.

**38. Utilitarian** ◆ *(→ minimalism)* — "Function-first, clean, and direct. It prioritizes clarity over aesthetics and often has a rugged, military-industrial feel."
· *Core:* Grid layout, monospaced or industrial fonts, muted tones, absence of decoration.
· *Mood:* Practical, minimal, efficient. Manuals, signage, utility apps, technical interfaces.

**39. Mid-Century** — 1940s–60s; form, function, organic simplicity. Bold geometry + warm textures.
· *Core:* Clean lines, retro colors, boomerangs, mod patterns, sans-serif fonts.
· *Mood:* Nostalgic, modernist, optimistic. Furniture branding, lifestyle blogs, editorial layouts.

**40. Scrapbook** ◆ *(→ hand-drawn)* — Handmade collaging of memories; personal, layered, nostalgic.
· *Core:* Paper textures, washi tape, Polaroid frames, hand lettering, stickers.
· *Mood:* Sentimental, warm, nostalgic. Journaling apps, memory branding, school projects.

**41. Neo Frutiger Aero** — Y2K revival sub-style: glossy UI, rounded sans-serifs like Frutiger, bubble gradients.
· *Core:* Aqua blues, semi-transparent bubbles, gloss overlays, friendly icons, rounded UI.
· *Mood:* Futuristic, nostalgic, clean. Tech, playful fintech, Web3 brands.

**42. Dark Magic Academia** — Gothic academia + mysticism; dark, intellectual, symbolic.
· *Core:* Candles, spellbooks, antique etchings, black/gold palette, serif calligraphy.
· *Mood:* Mysterious, scholarly, magical. Fantasy games, book clubs, witchy brands.

**43. Light Academia** — Soft, poetic, classical; romanticizes knowledge and literature.
· *Core:* Creamy neutrals, serif fonts, books, sunlight, linen textures.
· *Mood:* Scholarly, calm, refined. Journals, study apps, aesthetic fashion.

**44. Wabi Sabi** — Japanese philosophy of imperfection and impermanence; natural textures, asymmetry.
· *Core:* Earthy tones, rough pottery textures, natural light, organic forms, minimal sans-serif.
· *Mood:* Humble, calm, contemplative. Wellness brands, tea packaging, mindful interfaces.

**45. South West / Wild West** — American desert landscapes, cowboy culture, Native American influences.
· *Core:* Terracotta, cacti, denim textures, cowboy boots, serif western fonts.
· *Mood:* Rugged, adventurous, nostalgic. Ranch brands, outdoor gear, festival posters.

**46. Nautical** — Sea life, ships, coastal living; fresh and orderly.
· *Core:* Navy and white, anchors, ropes, stripes, brass, serif or stencil fonts.
· *Mood:* Fresh, structured, maritime. Seafood brands, beach resorts, summer campaigns.

**47. Rebus** — Communicates through symbols or images representing sounds or words.
· *Core:* Pictograms, visual puns, image-type hybrids, clean layouts.
· *Mood:* Witty, playful, intellectual. Educational design, puzzles, brand storytelling.

**48. Glassmorphism** ◆ — "Gives UI elements a frosted glass look — transparent with blur and depth. It's soft, futuristic, and layered."
· *Core:* Frosted backgrounds, blur effects, semi-transparency, soft shadows, neon tints.
· *Mood:* Futuristic, elegant, sleek. Apps, OS UI, product landing pages.

**49. Modular Typography** ◆ *(→ swiss)* — "Breaks type into grids or building blocks. It allows flexible systems and unique visual rhythm, often experimental in nature."
· *Core:* Geometric letterforms, typographic grids, uniform spacing, variable layouts.
· *Mood:* Structural, modern, playful. Posters, branding systems, experimental typography.

**50. Neo-Brutalism** ◆ — "A cleaner, more structured evolution of classic Brutalism. It retains raw honesty but adds better UI practices — useful in digital products that want to be bold yet functional."
· *Core:* Bold colors, large type, stark layouts, minimal UI components, purposeful asymmetry.
· *Mood:* Confident, bold, raw-yet-usable. Design portfolios, creative agency sites, digital magazines.

## Author's usage notes (verbatim points)

- **Prompt smarter** — "I now start my AI prompt with a style name, like 'Ethereal UI design' or
  'Neo-Brutalist web layout.' The AI instantly narrows its aesthetic."
- **Mix styles** — "Once you know the DNA of each style, you can create hybrids like 'Vaporwave x
  Gothic' or 'Luxury Typography meets Aurora.'"
- **Build moodboards faster** — "generate 3–4 styles for the same concept. Visual clarity = decision
  clarity."
- **Brief teams effectively** — "saying 'Make it feel like Art Nouveau meets Kawaii' gets your
  message across way faster than trying to describe a vibe."

## Verdict for our purposes

Best art-direction source of the four. Every entry is structured identically, which makes it the
natural schema for a style pack's `## art direction` half. **Contains zero implementation detail** —
no CSS, no measurements, no values. It is a prompting vocabulary, not a build spec.
