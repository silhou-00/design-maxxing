Stacking Cards

Panels stick in sequence and pile up at the top of the viewport. Built entirely from sticky offsets.

Binding native Mechanism position: sticky with an incremental offset Cost very low Risk low
01

Each panel sticks at a hard offset

18px per panel, not a smooth ramp. The offsets are visible and countable, which is the point — the mechanism is not hidden.

02

The shadow does the depth

Because the shadow has no blur, a panel landing on the one beneath reads as two solid planes rather than a soft pile.

03

No-shrink variant

Nothing scales and nothing fades. A scale() would round the corners optically and undo the pack; a fade is banned outright by it.

04

Built from position, not JavaScript

This is position: sticky and one custom property. No scroll listener, no library, no measurement on every frame.

The craft

The offset is a custom property, not four hand-written values. top: calc(12vh + var(--i) * 18px) means adding a fifth panel is one attribute, and the ramp cannot drift out of sequence.

The no-shrink variant is the one to reach for. The common version scales each panel down as the next arrives. That softens corners optically — which is fine for a rounded pack and destructive for a hard-edged one — and it is motion that must then collapse under reduced motion. Sticky alone collapses to nothing because nothing was moving.

The wrapper needs trailing space. Without padding-bottom the last panel unsticks the instant it arrives.

Implementation

.panel{
  position: sticky;
  top: calc(12vh + var(--i) * 18px);   /* one property, any number of panels */
}
/* <article class="panel" style="--i:0"> … --i:1, --i:2 */

/* the wrapper needs trailing space or the last panel unsticks immediately */
.stackwrap{ padding-bottom: 40vh }

When not to use it

  • With a scale or fade under reduced motion — sticky alone has nothing to collapse; added motion does.
  • On more than four or five panels. Past that the stack occupies more scroll than it earns.
  • In brutalism. Stacking is polish, and the pack rates it ✗.

Style packs

● for neo-brutalism — a 4px 4px 0 hard shadow makes the stack's depth explicit and literal in a way a soft shadow cannot — and bento, where cells and cards share a geometry. ✗ for brutalism, which rates it as polish.
