Scroll Theme Shift

The page's colours change as you scroll. Solid swaps only; the moment it becomes a gradient it is a different technique.

Ink to primary, as you cross

One solid value interpolating to another. No gradient, no blur — which is exactly why flat rates this ● and why it costs almost nothing.

The contrast moves with it

This is the trap. Text must clear contrast at both ends of the range and everywhere between. Shifting from a light ground to a dark one leaves white text at 1.15:1 for the first half — a real failure caught by the audit on this repo's own flat page.

Give it a base colour

With keyframes alone the element is transparent until the timeline resolves, so text lands on the page ground for a frame. Declare the from colour as a real background.

The craft

The contrast changes with the background, so it must be checked across the whole range — not at one end. A shift from the page ground to a primary leaves white text unreadable for the first half of the scroll. Shift dark → dark, or light → light, and the text stays legible throughout.

Always declare the from colour as a real background. With keyframes alone the element is transparent until the timeline resolves; text then sits on the page ground for a frame, and any contrast check sees the wrong pair.

Neumorphism rates this ✗ and the reason is mechanical: its dark shadow is the surface × 0.87 and its light shadow is the surface + 13%. Animate the background and both shadows become wrong, so the extrusion inverts or vanishes mid-scroll.

Implementation

.shift{
  background-color: #2c3e50;         /* the FROM colour, declared for real */
  animation: th auto linear both;
  animation-timeline: view();
  animation-range: cover 10% cover 78%;
}
@keyframes th{ from{ background-color:#2c3e50 } to{ background-color:#20628f } }

@supports not (animation-timeline: view()){
  .shift{ background-color:#2c3e50; animation:none }
}

When not to use it

  • Shifting between a light and a dark ground with fixed text colour — contrast breaks mid-range.
  • In neumorphism, where both shadows are derived from the surface and would need recomputing every frame.
  • As a gradient. A soft fade is a different technique and most packs that allow this one ban that.

Style packs

● for flat — solid colour swaps with no gradients are exactly what the style is made of. ✗ for neumorphism (both shadows derive from the surface) and minimalism, which bans a second visual event. Note this is one of two techniques that legitimately animate a non-composite property; it is cheap because background-color paints without layout.
