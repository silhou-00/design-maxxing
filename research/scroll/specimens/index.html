01 · scroll progress scroll-progress ↗

Reading indicator

The orange bar at the very top. A Scroll Progress Timeline on the root scroller, scaling a fixed bar on the X axis. animation-timeline: scroll(). Scale, not width — width would trigger layout on every frame.

02 · scroll reveal scroll-reveal ↗

Entrance on enter

A View Progress Timeline per card, confined to animation-range: entry 10% cover 38% so each finishes well before it reaches the middle of the screen. The single most-used scroll effect on the web.

Card one — fades and lifts as it enters.
Card two — same keyframes, its own timeline.
Card three — no stagger logic needed; position in the scrollport is the stagger.

03 · parallax parallax ↗

Layers at different rates

Two layers on one view timeline, translating at 16% and 52% of the range. Transform-only, so it composites off the main thread. WCAG 2.3.3 names parallax explicitly as non-essential motion that can trigger vestibular reactions — it is killed under prefers-reduced-motion below.

04 · pinned scene pinned-scene ↗

Sticky stage, scrubbed contents

A 260vh parent carries the timeline; a position: sticky child is the stage. The box rotates, scales and morphs its radius across contain — the range where the tall parent fully covers the scrollport. No JS pinning, no pin-spacer, no layout shift.

05 · stacking cards stacking-cards ↗

Sticky stack with depth

Each card is position: sticky; top: 14vh. As a card exits, the exit-crossing range scales it down and darkens it, so the stack reads as depth rather than as overlap.

06 · horizontal scroll  +  07 · scroll snap horizontal-scroll ↗ scroll-snap ↗

A real horizontal scroller

Native overflow-x: auto with scroll-snap-type: x mandatory. The rail below is driven by a named timeline on the inline axis (scroll-timeline: --rail inline). Note this stays keyboard- and touch-native — unlike the JS pattern that converts vertical scroll into horizontal movement.

08 · text reveal text-reveal ↗

Each word is its own subject on its own view timeline.

Blur is the expensive part here — it is a paint-level filter, not a composited transform. Fine on eleven words, not fine on a paragraph.

09 · sticky header morph

Range-limited scrub

The header above shrinks and gains a background over the first 200px only: animation-range: 0 200px. A fixed-length range, not a percentage — the cleanest way to bind an effect to an absolute scroll distance.

10 · scroll-driven theme shift scroll-theme-shift ↗

Background bound to section progress

Three keyframe stops across cover. Cheap, since background-color animates on the compositor in modern engines — but it is the one effect here that can silently destroy text contrast. Check every stop.

11 · scroll marquee scroll-marquee ↗

POSITION-BOUND · NOT TIME-BOUND · REVERSES ON SCROLL UP · POSITION-BOUND · NOT TIME-BOUND ·

The difference from a CSS-animation marquee: this one is still when the page is still, and runs backwards when you scroll up. Motion that reports scroll rather than demanding attention.

12 · scroll shadows

Edge cue that knows when to leave

The bottom fade disappears over the last 15% of the inner scroller, so it never lies about there being more content. The most useful scroll-driven effect that nobody calls an animation.
